### Review (verbatim)

1. **Overall assessment**

LLP 0028 proposes the right thing at the right time. The direction — one Oxc engine, zero SWC crates, one fingerprint domain — is already accepted policy (LLP 0009's Decision, LLP 0026's Phase 5), the module runner has been the ordinary-ESM default since ENG-25066, and the repo facts the RFC leans on mostly check out: the nine `swc_*` crates are exactly the nine in `Cargo.toml:110-118`, the Rust pin is 1.93.1 (`rust-toolchain.toml`), the Oxc pin is `=0.121.0` (`Cargo.toml:54-62`), the version fence and kill switch are real (`src/bin/ibex/runtime.rs:26-37`), the cache tags are verbatim (`src/module_loader/transpile.rs:50-51`), the Tier 2 scanner and the two conformance runners exist as described, and `surface.loader.*.swc.*` IDs are present in `src/capsec_registry_generated.rs` (e.g. lines 4067-4251). The rollout order (toolchain first, shapes second, gate third, window close, then surgery) is sensible and each step is behind an existing measured gate. As a program skeleton, this is good work.

The RFC is weakest exactly where it claims to be strongest: "the handful of *new* decisions the existing documents left open." Each of the three new decisions is underdone. The parser-equivalence section (§3) mischaracterizes what LLP 0024's gate protects and proposes an oracle (the bundled Hermes parser) that cannot cover the gated property, because Hermes never parses the TypeScript dialect the gate pins. The three-shape disposition (§2) is internally inconsistent — the rationale given for failing computed `require()` closed applies verbatim to computed `import()`, which the RFC supports — and its computed-import semantics silently diverges from LLP 0026 §6's authenticated-candidate-map contract. And the claim that after the three shapes are resolved "IBEX_LEGACY_MODULE_LOADER has nothing left to guard" is contradicted by the code: the compatibility evaluator also serves unadvertised targets (Windows), snapshotless audit runtimes, and non-script/.hbc entry fallbacks, none of which the RFC dispositions. The surgery plan in §4 likewise misses live SWC call sites outside the selector.

None of this changes the verdict on the *idea* — retire SWC, yes, now — but an RFC whose stated purpose is closing the open decisions has to actually close them, and two of the three are closed with reasoning that doesn't survive contact with the governing documents and code.

2. **Strengths**

- **Correct framing of authority** (Summary, Non-goals): the RFC executes LLP 0009/0026 rather than re-litigating, and explicitly names the LLP 0026 allowance it supersedes (the parser-only SWC carve-out, LLP 0026 Phase 5 "a parser-only SWC dependency may outlive the module-transform role") instead of quietly contradicting it. Superseding that carve-out is the right instinct — a retained parser-only dependency preserves the double-conformance surface where divergence is least observable (Alternatives §1).
- **Verified factual grounding** (Motivation, §1, §4): the toolchain matrix matches LLP 0009's Context verbatim (`rolldown 1.1.1` → Oxc 0.135.0 → Rust 1.94.0); the fence, kill switch, cache tags, crate list, and capsec surface IDs are all accurate against the tree; the three legacy shapes match the actual `LegacyRequired` raise sites (`src/module_loader/producer_spike.rs:820,940,945`; `runner_pipeline.rs:362-365`).
- **Gated, ordered rollout** (§5): toolchain bump validated by corpus plus the real performance-gate report id (`ibex/module-runner-performance-gate/1`, LLP 0026's envelopes) before any engine surgery; zero-divergence applied per landing; per-step Linear issues with same-commit LLP updates matches repo convention.
- **TypeScript commitment restated** (Summary, Non-goals): explicitly preserving full-TS lowering (enums, namespaces) and rejecting an erasable-syntax-only narrowing without its own Decision correctly guards LLP 0009's "TypeScript runtime direction" against a tempting scope cut.
- **Auditable removal** (§4): regenerating `src/capsec_registry_generated.rs` so the `swc` surface IDs disappear from the inventory turns the deletion into a checkable record — a genuinely nice use of the capsec registry.

3. **Concerns**

- **Material — §3 mis-states what LLP 0024's parser-equivalence gate protects, and the proposed replacement oracle cannot cover it.** LLP 0024 pins the *session TypeScript dialect* to `swc_ecma_parser` at the `Cargo.lock` version because "dialect drift (`satisfies`, `using`, decorators) silently changes the completeness judgments of §5," and admits Oxc "only while it is parse-equivalent to the pinned parser over the conformance corpus" (LLP 0024, lines 676-683 and the "Parse equivalence" fixture-family row). The premises there are TypeScript's, not Hermes's: types are stripped before Hermes ever sees the code, and the bundled `tools/hermes/hermes` parser cannot serve as an oracle for `satisfies`, decorators, or TSX at all. §3's claim that "the premises are Hermes's… agreement between those two is the property the gate exists to prove" is wrong for the gate it cites. An Oxc-vs-Hermes acceptance check is a fine *additional* JS-goal gate; it is not a substitute. *Resolution:* rewrite §3 as a two-part contract — (a) a normative LLP 0024 revision re-pinning the named parser to `oxc_parser` at its locked version, with a one-time SWC-vs-Oxc parse-equivalence run over LLP 0024's corpus (every goal and dialect) as the cutover evidence, and (b) optionally the Hermes-acceptance check for JS goals as a new, separately-motivated gate. Also name LLP 0026 §3's *third* SWC gate — LLP 0024's session *lowering* stage is contractually built on SWC — since §3 currently calls the parser gate "the last named blocker," which contradicts LLP 0026's three-gate structure.

- **Material — the computed-`require()` / computed-`import()` asymmetry is unjustified as written, and contradicts LLP 0026.** §2's rationale for failing computed `require()` closed — "statically unanalyzable, which breaks both LLP 0014 policy generation and LLP 0027 edge provenance" — applies identically to computed dynamic `import()`, which the same section supports natively. LLP 0026 explicitly models computed `require()` as a dynamic edge "resolved and authorized when execution reaches it" (Terminology; §1 "Dynamic `import()` and computed `require()` repeat the applicable resolution and policy checks at call time"; §4's single authorization operation covers "literal and computed `require()`"), so 0028 is quietly reversing 0026's design, with an argument that its own adjacent paragraph disproves. Computed `require()` is also pervasive in real CJS (plugin loaders, `bindings`-style native resolution, driver selection); failing it closed is a real ecosystem cut. *Resolution:* either (a) support both through the same call-time gate (computed `require()` additionally needing the §6/§7 sync-drive async-taint proof — machinery LLP 0026 already specifies), or (b) keep the fail-closed decision but justify it with a rationale that actually distinguishes the two (and ideally with usage evidence from the 0.1 window's `LegacyRequired` warnings), and name it as an explicit LLP 0026 §1/§4 amendment.

- **Material — computed-import semantics diverge from LLP 0026 §6 without saying so.** §2 says computed specifiers "resolve at runtime through the same authenticated resolver and admission path." LLP 0026 §6's contract is narrower: computed sites "may select only from the exact authenticated candidate map supplied with the graph; a spelling not in that map becomes a rejected promise without a resolution or filesystem probe" — and the shipped graph code implements exactly the candidate-map shape (`src/module_loader/graph.rs:353-397`, `computed_candidate`). Open call-time resolution and a closed candidate table are different security contracts; the RFC's motivating patterns (route tables, locale files computed from data) are also the ones a build-time candidate map may not enumerate. *Resolution:* pick one semantics; if open call-time resolution within admitted policy, mark it as an explicit LLP 0026 §6 revision with its non-probe/non-disclosure story; if candidate-map, explain how LLP 0014 policy generation produces the map for a computed site and show the motivating patterns actually fit.

- **Material — "the window has nothing left to guard" is contradicted by the fence code; Windows and snapshotless runtimes have no disposition.** The compatibility evaluator serves more than the three shapes: (a) unadvertised targets — only macOS arm64 and Linux x64 are advertised (`runtime.rs:42-44`), and with the window closed an unadvertised target hard-fails script entries (`runtime.rs:1451-1458`), so at 0.2 with SWC deleted, Windows (LLP 0026: "compatibility-only pending a patched Hermes artifact") loses runtime TS/ESM execution entirely; (b) audit/diagnostic runtimes with no armed snapshot "retain the compatibility evaluator" with no window check at all (`runtime.rs:1445-1450`); (c) non-script `.hbc` entries and their stale-bytecode source fallback route around the runner and can reach the SWC entry shim (`runtime.rs:1823`). The RFC dispositions none of these. *Resolution:* add a disposition for each legacy-evaluator consumer that is not one of the three shapes — concretely, either make Windows advertisement (patched Hermes artifact) or an explicit Windows de-support Decision a named prerequisite of step 4, and state what snapshotless runtimes and `.hbc` fallbacks run on after surgery.

- **Material — §4's surgery misses live SWC call sites, so "the Oxc path becomes the only body" is not executable as written.** `run_entry_with_tla_shim` calls `transpile_to_cjs` (SWC) directly for every source-extension entry it lowers (`runtime.rs:1915`), and is reachable outside the window-gated shapes (see previous concern); the shipped Oxc body meanwhile *bails* on general ESM lowering and TLA (`transpile.rs:212-217, 258-263`) — precisely what those callers need. §4 mentions only the `already_lowered` comments in `runtime.rs`. The `EXACT_TRANSPILE_SCRIPT` subprocess override (`mod.rs:2103,2370,2595`) and the `contains_top_level_await` CLI text scanner (which LLP 0026 Phase 5 explicitly retires with the scanner tier) are also undispositioned. *Resolution:* add a removal inventory to §4 enumerating every `transpile.rs` consumer (`runtime.rs:1915`, `mod.rs:2120/2377`, the subprocess override) and the entry-TLA shim/text scanner, each with "retires with the window," "moves to the runner producer," or "explicitly retained."

- **Minor — the Motivation overstates the current duplication.** "Every hermes-compat pass … currently exists in an SWC flavor and an Oxc flavor" is not accurate: the SWC path applies no Hermes-compat passes at all (LLP 0007 "Current state"; `transpile.rs` contains none); the tiers are LLP 0019's JS-AST authority, bootstrap scanner, and Rust/Oxc mirror. Diagnostics and cache-key domains do exist in two engine flavors; the compat passes do not. *Resolution:* reword to what is duplicated (engines, cache domains, diagnostics) versus what is tiered (LLP 0019 passes).

- **Minor — "preserved bit-for-bit against the LLP 0019 conformance corpus" is the wrong discipline.** LLP 0019's central lesson is that shape/text assertions prove nothing and only engine-honest *behavioral* fixtures gate transforms; LLP 0026 likewise gates "by observable behavior, not transformed text." An Oxc re-pin will change bytes; the corpus will (correctly) not notice. *Resolution:* replace "bit-for-bit" with "behaviorally, on real Hermes, per the corpus."

- **Minor — the loader-path conformance runner loses its system under test at step 4.** `run-hermes-compat-loader.mjs` drives fixtures through the in-process pipeline the Tier 2 scanner serves; §5 says both runners "remain the gate at every step," but after Tier 2 retires the loader runner either retires with it or must be repointed at the runner pipeline. *Resolution:* one sentence in §5 stating the runner's post-retirement disposition.

- **Minor — the fingerprint "rotation" is smaller than described.** `transform_fingerprint` already names only Oxc (`producer_spike.rs:73-75`, `parser_version: "oxc-0.121.0"`); the change is a version-string bump, not a vocabulary change. "Parser/transform version components now name only Oxc" implies they currently name SWC. *Resolution:* reword.

- **Minor — the toolchain matrix is a month old.** LLP 0009's Rust/Oxc matrix is dated 2026-06-14; "≥ 1.94.0" and "Oxc 0.135.0" may already be stale for a "current release" re-pin in late July. *Resolution:* state that step 1 re-measures the matrix rather than inheriting it.

4. **Suggestions**

- **Use the 0.1 window as telemetry before closing it.** The fallback already prints a typed reason per `LegacyRequired` admission (`runtime.rs:1573-1578`); aggregating those reasons from CI and downstream (Snapback) runs would turn the computed-`require()` disposition from taste into evidence, and would surface any fourth shape nobody remembers.
- **Make the parse-equivalence evidence a one-shot archived artifact, not a retained dependency.** Run `oxc_parser` vs `swc_ecma_parser` differentially over LLP 0024's corpus plus a large real-world sweep (the transpile cache of a representative app's `node_modules`) once at cutover, archive the report, then re-pin LLP 0024 to Oxc. That answers the "third opinion" alternative more convincingly than the Hermes framing.
- **Derive the cache tag from the fingerprint digest** rather than hand-versioning `in-process-oxc-<ver>-vN`, so a future re-pin cannot forget to rotate the tag; adopt the quarterly-max pin-rotation cadence from the open question as a short Guide-type LLP shared with the Rust toolchain policy.
- **For computed dynamic import, consider "policy-closure candidate" semantics**: candidate set = every module the importer's armed policy already admits, materialized at graph build. It reconciles LLP 0026 §6's closed-map contract with §2's "no new authority" framing and handles route-table/locale patterns whose spellings are data-driven but package-local.
- Name the Snapback LLP 0062 dependency as unverifiable-from-this-repo (it is cross-repo) and pin the exact claim Snapback needs ("one engine, one fingerprint domain") as an acceptance criterion the program can check.
- Add explicit acceptance criteria and a `Status`/gating section like LLP 0026's — the RFC currently has rollout steps but no falsifiable "this RFC is complete when" list.

5. **Open questions**

- What is Windows x64's fate at 0.2: patched-Hermes advertisement as a prerequisite of window close, or an explicit de-support Decision? (Currently unanswered and blocking step 4.)
- What do snapshotless audit/diagnostic runtimes and `.hbc`-entry stale-bytecode fallbacks execute on after the legacy evaluator and SWC are gone?
- Computed `import()`: authenticated candidate map (LLP 0026 §6 as written) or open call-time resolution (LLP 0026 amendment)? And how does LLP 0014 policy generation feed whichever is chosen?
- Computed `require()`: what is the real-world frequency in the window's `LegacyRequired` telemetry, and does any downstream (Snapback agent-written code, Exact packages) depend on it?
- What exactly does the revised LLP 0024 pin look like — parser identity, version-assertion mechanism (the build-time check LLP 0024 promises does not exist yet), and the migration of its SWC-based session-lowering contract?
- The RFC's own three: the exact Oxc pin and rotation cadence; whether the `IBEX_RUNTIME_TRANSFORM` deprecation window is worth it; whether any consumer needs a mapped diagnostic-code table for the retired SWC error strings — plus the disposition of `EXACT_TRANSPILE_SCRIPT`.
- What does the loader-path conformance runner test after Tier 2 retires?

6. **Readiness verdict**

VERDICT: NOT READY — the program direction and rollout skeleton are sound, but the RFC's three "new decisions" contain material defects: the LLP 0024 gate replacement substitutes an oracle that cannot cover the gated TypeScript-dialect property, the computed-require/computed-import disposition is internally inconsistent and silently amends LLP 0026 §6, and the window-close and surgery plans omit real legacy-evaluator consumers (Windows/unadvertised targets, snapshotless runtimes, the SWC entry-TLA shim) that the code shows are live.

### Review (verbatim)

1. **Overall assessment**

LLP 0028 is a strong, unusually honest execution RFC for a direction the corpus has already accepted (LLP 0009 settled Oxc; LLP 0026 demoted SWC to a bounded 0.1 compatibility engine). Its load-bearing factual claims check out against the tree almost without exception: the Rust pin is 1.93.1 (`rust-toolchain.toml`, mirrored in `.github/workflows/ci.yml:20` and `module-loader-baselines.yml:67`); the nine `swc_*` crates and the Oxc 0.121.0 pins are exactly as described (`Cargo.toml:54-62, 110-118`); the `TransformEngine` selector, cache tags `in-process-swc-v2` / `in-process-oxc-0.121.0-v1`, and the Oxc TLA/ESM-lowering bails are real (`src/module_loader/transpile.rs:41-95, 212-263`); the three `LegacyRequired` shapes are precisely the three the code produces (`src/module_loader/runner_pipeline.rs:352-367`, `src/module_loader/producer_spike.rs:819-829, 939-948`), including the transformed-byte-offset defect the RFC promises to fix (`producer_spike.rs:941`); the entry TLA shim lowers every source extension through SWC outside the window gate (`src/bin/ibex/runtime.rs:1892-1943`); the snapshotless audit path retains the compat evaluator without a window check (`runtime.rs:1445-1450`); the capsec generator really does discover engines via `TransformEngine::` token references and throws when none exist (`capsec-surface-inventory.mjs:12424-12439`), which validates the RFC's insistence on generator-before-deletion sequencing; and the spike manifest really has exactly one TSX fixture. The document's self-corrections (the round-1 rewrite of the LLP 0024 gate treatment, the admission that Hermes cannot oracle the TS dialect) are correct and materially improve on the naive version of this program.

The plan's shape is right: inventory-plus-negative-gate instead of review-time search, atomic pin/identity rotation, explicit platform decision instead of silent Windows de-support, and telemetry-gated deferral of computed `require`. Three things keep it from being ready. First, the sequencing puts the largest engine change (the Oxc version jump, step 1) ahead of the corpora that actually exercise the native path — the native Tier 3 runner and the behavioral transform corpus arrive later, while today no runner drives the LLP 0019 corpus through the native producer at all and the native for-of mirror already diverges from the Tier 1 authority. Second, the §3(c) gate chains SWC crate deletion to "reimplementing" a session-lowering stage that, as far as the code shows, does not exist yet — SWC appears only in `transpile.rs` — and LLP 0026 §3 explicitly offers contract revision as the alternative gate, which the RFC never weighs; relatedly, the §3(b) differential run is specified over a session goal that LLP 0024 §3 says neither parser supplies today. Third, the default package-closure candidate map quietly converts today's fail-closed deny of computed import into ambient within-policy selection at every computed site, and the RFC's "no new authority" argument, while true at the package level, skips the review-surface and attacker-controlled-selection deltas.

None of these require re-litigating the direction; all three have concrete, bounded fixes. With them addressed this would be a model retirement RFC.

2. **Strengths**

- **Scope honesty (§ Summary, § Non-goals, §4b).** The "one engine" claim is explicitly fenced against the two surfaces that would otherwise falsify it: the build-time Babel `--lower-classes` stage (verified live in `package.json:10` and recorded in LLP 0007 "Current state") and `EXACT_TRANSPILE_SCRIPT` (verified live with its own cache identity, `src/module_loader/mod.rs:2103, 2599`). Classifying the Babel stage in the retirement manifest as *named-out-of-scope* is exactly the right move.
- **Correct three-gate decomposition (§3).** The RFC's recovery from "parser gate is the last blocker, satisfy it Oxc-vs-Hermes" is accurate: LLP 0026 §3 names three independent gates, LLP 0024 pins `swc_ecma_parser` for the session dialect (LLP 0024 lines 677-683), and Hermes genuinely cannot oracle TS. Keeping the Oxc-vs-Hermes acceptance check as *additional, not substitute* is well judged.
- **Atomic pin/identity rotation (§1).** The hazard is real and verified: the Oxc version is hard-coded in at least three places (`transpile.rs:51`, `producer_spike.rs:44`, `producer_spike.rs:74`) with no shared constant. Requiring rotation goldens (pre-rotation cache miss, prepared-carrier revalidate-or-rebuild) matches the LLP 0027 fingerprint contract.
- **Computed-edge dispositions conform rather than amend silently (§2).** The candidate-map semantics match LLP 0026 §6's authenticated-map contract verbatim, the wire hook exists (`DynamicEdgeV1::Computed { site }`, LLP 0027 lines 63-66, 185-187), and graph-side machinery is already partially in place (`graph.rs:353-397` and the `computed_dynamic_import_uses_only_the_authenticated_candidate_set` test). The computed-`require` amendment is argued on genuinely distinguishing grounds (sync drive machinery; no CJS attribute channel — confirmed by LLP 0014 line 121), labeled as an amendment, and given a telemetry-based reopening trigger. Fixing the build-time/transformed-offset rejection to an invocation-time original-source error is a real correctness improvement over `producer_spike.rs:939-943`.
- **Reachability inventory matches the code (§4b).** Every row I checked is real: the shim (`runtime.rs:1892+`), the audit path (`runtime.rs:1445`), the `.hbc` fallback reaching the shim (`runtime.rs:1823`), the bootstrap rewrites (`module-loader.js:3287, 4094, 4661+`), and the capsec IDs (`capsec_registry_generated.rs:4067-4251`).
- **§4d refuses to make a platform decision by side effect.** The refusal behavior it describes is exactly what ships (`runtime.rs:1451-1457`), and LLP 0001's revision history confirms Windows is a compatibility-only row.
- **§5's runner-gap diagnosis is accurate.** `tests/hermes_compat_conformance.rs` wires exactly two runners, the loader runner uses `EXACT_COMPAT_TEST=1` (`run-hermes-compat-loader.mjs:180`), and the native for-of rewrite handles only the single-identifier const/let block shape (`producer_spike.rs:428-472`). Repointing the loader runner instead of retiring it silently is the right instinct.

3. **Concerns**

- **[Material] Native-path conformance arrives after the change it should gate.** Step 1 rotates the production engine under the default path (module runner default since ENG-25066; `Cargo.toml:124`), but the only real-Hermes coverage of the *native* path today is the 12-fixture spike manifest plus module-runner tests — the LLP 0019 corpus runners exercise Tier 1 and the compat loader, not the native mirror (`tests/hermes_compat_conformance.rs:120-133`), and the behavioral transform corpus is "a named workstream" with one TSX fixture existing. Worse, a live Tier 3 divergence already exists: the native mirror leaves any non-simple for-of shape (destructured binding, `var`, non-block body) raw (`producer_spike.rs:429-438`), where the Tier 1 authority rewrites "any parseable loop" (LLP 0019 "Accepted divergences") and LLP 0019 declares Tier 3 a *zero-divergence* mirror that is now canonical. The RFC names the gap but sequences its detection apparatus (native Tier 3 runner) only as a pre-Tier-2-retirement gate (step 4 territory), and step 1's "full conformance corpus" is ambiguous about which corpus. *Resolution:* sequence the native Tier 3 runner (and at least a first tranche of the behavioral corpus) before or with step 1, state explicitly which corpora gate step 1, and record the existing for-of divergence in LLP 0019 as a live, dated exception until fixed.
- **[Material] §3(c) treats an unbuilt stage as a migration and ignores LLP 0026's revision alternative.** SWC appears in exactly one implementation file (`src/module_loader/transpile.rs`; `runtime.rs` references are comments, the registry is generated). LLP 0024 is Status: Draft, and its own text says the session source goal "does not exist in the pinned parser today" (lines 444-456) and that the transform "does not emit source maps today" (line 422). There is no shipped SWC session-lowering stage to "reimplement." LLP 0026 §3 explicitly allows the alternative — "SWC crates leave the tree only when all three gates pass (**or 0024's contracts are revised**)" (LLP 0026 lines 535-536) — and revising a Draft spec's engine naming so the session stack is built Oxc-native from the start is dramatically cheaper than gating crate deletion on landing the full LLP 0024 lowering program. Compounding this, the §3(b) differential run promises coverage of "the hybrid Script-plus-`import`-plus-TLA session goal that neither plain Script nor Module parsing supplies" — per LLP 0024 open question 5, *neither parser implements that goal*, so the differential as specified cannot execute for it. *Resolution:* re-scope §3(c) as an LLP 0024 revision (pin `oxc_parser`, name Oxc as the session-lowering engine) with the fixture families gating the *future* session implementation, and define what the differential run actually parses for the hybrid goal (e.g., the chosen OQ5 mechanism on both stacks, or scope the differential to implemented goals).
- **[Material] The package-closure candidate-map default widens computed-import reach without per-site review.** Today computed dynamic import is denied outright (LLP 0014: "Computed/obfuscated specifiers contribute nothing and are denied at runtime," lines 147-148, 232; enforced at `runner_pipeline.rs:364`). §2's *default* gives every computed site the importer package's entire admitted import set. "No new authority" is true package-level, but the delta is real: attacker-influenced *data* (the computed string) can now select and initialize any module in the package's closure — including side-effectful initializers that would otherwise never load — and future additions to the package's import set silently widen every computed site, outside LLP 0014's `--check` expansion-tripwire framing, which reviews edges, not per-site selection sets. *Resolution:* either make explicit declaration the default and package-closure opt-in, or keep the default but require the materialized per-site candidate map to appear in the generated policy artifact and in `--check` diffs (so closure growth at a computed site is a reviewable expansion), and state the threat-model argument in §2.
- **[Minor] "All nine Oxc crates" miscounts.** §1 names eight version-locked crates plus `oxc_sourcemap`/`oxc_resolver` "on their own tracks" — that is 8 + 2 = 10 in `Cargo.toml:53-62`, not nine. (The "nine `swc_*` crates" count in §4c is correct.) *Resolution:* fix the count or say "eight lockstep crates plus two independently-tracked ones."
- **[Minor] §2's import-options bullet misstates which attributes are accepted.** "The attribute subset LLP 0014 reserves for grants (today `type: "json"` …) is accepted" is wrong as written: LLP 0014's grant-reserved keys (`grants`, `endow`, `builtins`, `also`) are build-time-only and *rejected* on the wire (LLP 0027 lines 70-71: "Reserved LLP 0014 policy keys are rejected"); what the runtime accepts is the standard attribute `type: "json"` (`identity.rs:234`). *Resolution:* reword to "the runtime attribute subset (today `type: "json"`), with LLP 0014-reserved policy keys rejected as LLP 0027 already prescribes."
