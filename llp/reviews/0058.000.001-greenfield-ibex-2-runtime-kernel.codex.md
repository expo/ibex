Overall assessment: the bounded-greenfield direction is strong, but the Spec is not yet decision-complete. Six material issues leave bootstrap security, module identity, lifecycle ownership, artifact isolation, provenance, and production graduation open to divergent implementations.

## Findings

### C1 — Bootstrap order contradicts the governing protocol

- **Target:** §6 steps 4–6.
- **Governing evidence:** LLP 0058.000.000 §6.1 requires construction roots to be captured, deleted, and the integrity freeze completed before module bindings and namespaces are created.
- **Failure mode:** The target creates bindings and namespaces while construction-private roots remain installed and intrinsics remain unfrozen. Those namespaces can retain prototype or constructor relationships outside the verified frozen graph. This also directly contradicts a contract the target says it does not supersede.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Reorder §6 to match LLP 0058.000.000 exactly: capture adapters, delete and verify construction roots, perform lockdown, then create/cache module contexts, bindings, and namespaces through captured native state.

### C2 — The vertical-slice gate precedes its mandatory security prerequisites

- **Target:** §7, G2, and G3.
- **Failure mode:** G2 requires the slice to use real `FactoryV1`, authenticated module-bound timers and filesystem authority, production lockdown, and the real driver. Yet G3 is where authenticated module contexts, namespace caching, FactoryV1/HBC receipts, and deterministic lockdown are first implemented. G2 therefore either uses a diagnostic/synthetic shortcut forbidden by §6, or silently implements much of G3 early, making both gates non-objective.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Move the minimum single-module authenticated graph claim, FactoryV1 receipt verification, context minting, namespace construction, and lockdown into G2 prerequisites. Recast G3 as multi-module graph/import/re-export expansion and hostile-module conformance.

### C3 — Driver, engine-root, service-resource, and teardown ownership are not closed topologically

- **Target:** §§3.1–3.5 and §6.
- **Governing evidence:** LLP 0058.000.000 §§3.4, 8, and 9 require kernel-owned transitions while engine roots remain owner-thread-owned and transport resources remain safely retained through shutdown.
- **Failure mode:** The kernel owns callback/resource records and is the only component allowed to begin teardown, but it may contain neither engine roots nor platform transport objects. The adapter owns roots and engine destruction; services own transport; no component or port owns the complete owner-thread driver and teardown transaction. Implementers could consequently release Hermes before callback roots, recycle records before adapter/service acknowledgement, commit a task after `Closing`, or introduce cyclic kernel/adapter dependencies.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Add an explicit owner-thread runtime coordinator and kernel-defined engine/service ports. Specify that kernel records hold opaque adapter/service slot keys, define reserve/commit/rollback acknowledgements, and require a teardown barrier: kernel enters `Closing`, services dispose or detach, adapter releases all engine roots, Hermes is destroyed, then the kernel may close/recycle records.

### C4 — The unit of no-hybrid enforcement is ambiguous for dual-family products

- **Target:** §2, §3.5, §5, §9, and G6.
- **Failure mode:** The Spec permits a product to compile both families and select one before allocation, while §5 requires link scans to reject legacy paths and says a legacy path present in the linked artifact fails. If scans cover only a greenfield sublibrary, the final product shell can reintroduce a reachable legacy bridge. If they cover the final executable, a deliberately dual-family executable cannot pass. “Whole artifact” therefore has two incompatible interpretations.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Define the exact final-link and receipt boundary. The simplest safe rule is that a secure-selectable greenfield product artifact excludes all legacy runtime objects and exports, while the patched family ships as a separate artifact. If dual-family final binaries are required, specify a family-tagged opaque runtime handle, authenticated dispatch table, and reachability inventory from each constructor rather than relying on sublibrary scans.

### C5 — `upstream-pinned` and Factory/HBC provenance are not yet independently representable

- **Target:** §3.3, §5, G0, G5, and §10.
- **Capsule evidence:** Current provenance validation recognizes `source-patched`, `windows-source-patched`, and `android-maven`; it reconstructs checked-in build authorities, requires a nonempty patch stack, and binds selected runtime/link artifacts.
- **Failure mode:** The target introduces an empty-patch `upstream-pinned` receipt without specifying its versioned authority or validator. The module receipt binds a bytecode version but not explicitly the exact `hermesc` artifact, lowering implementation, relevant compiler flags, or build authority. A stale or differently configured compiler sharing the same bytecode version could therefore produce an accepted artifact whose FactoryV1 or language assumptions differ.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Make a new receipt schema and independent validator a G0 deliverable. Bind the exact upstream source-tree identity, canonical empty patch manifest, build/lowering authority, compiler binary and flags, target/runtime and link artifacts, HBC ABI, and final source/link manifests. Existing patched or Maven receipt classes must not be relabeled.

### C6 — Production graduation is not bound to an objective declared tier

- **Target:** G4–G6 and §10.
- **Failure mode:** `AdoptLeaf`, `WrapService`, `RewriteBoundary`, and `Absent` are allowed, while “declared application/API tier” has no normative manifest. An implementation could narrow the tier after testing, declare an empty or slice-only corpus, or promote the same engine receipt in one environment without carrying the evidence that made it production-eligible.
- **Classification:** MATERIAL.
- **Smallest sound disposition:** Define a receipt-bound graduation manifest containing the exact required API/operation set, module formats and graph, application corpus, postures, targets/features, permitted absences, source/link closure, and referenced LLP 0021 target-advertisement/conformance reports. G6 should require every required entry to pass and the product composition root to authenticate that exact manifest before selection.

## Reconstructed contracts

### 1. Runtime construction and ownership

The intended contract is:

- The product composition root chooses one runtime family before allocation and constructs the kernel, public-Hermes adapter, and typed platform services.
- The Rust kernel exclusively owns policy, generations, authority records, resource-stage commits, the host-task FIFO, the outer driver, and teardown.
- The adapter owns Hermes and engine-root slots; services own transport slots. Kernel records contain only opaque slot keys.
- Adapter and services call the kernel’s typed operation interface. The coordinator calls JS-free `checkpoint`, `invoke`, `release`, and `dispose` hooks.
- A host task is reserved, then either committed, rolled back to the same FIFO position, or terminally disposed according to LLP 0058.000.000 §8.
- Bootstrap follows §6’s nine-step sequence: receipt authentication, allocation with jobs disabled, pristine capture, private-adapter installation/capture/deletion, freeze, context and namespace construction, final inventory verification, `FactoryV1` invocation, then job execution.
- Teardown enters `Closing`, disposes service slots, releases engine roots, destroys Hermes, and only then closes and recycles kernel records (§3.1).

### 2. Artifact and provenance

The intended contract has these layers:

- A source-built, empty-patch public-Hermes input receives an independently validated `ibex/hermes-upstream-pinned-receipt/1`; Android Maven artifacts are ineligible.
- The module builder authenticates the graph and produces only receipt-bound `FactoryV1` source/HBC. Its receipt binds code, graph, factory format, binding profile, stdlib surface, compiler/lowering identity, and HBC ABI (§3.3).
- Patched and greenfield runtimes are separate final artifacts. A secure greenfield artifact contains no reachable legacy runtime object, export, bridge, or effect path (§5, §9).
- A receipt-bound graduation manifest declares the exact tier and all evidence required to select the artifact (§10).
- Product selection authenticates that manifest before allocation and remains immutable for the runtime generation (§3.5, G6).

### 3. Gates and dependencies

| Gate | Consumes | Delivers |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Candidate upstream pin, public-interface requirements, selected root inventory | Public-interface feasibility evidence, source-built engine artifacts, upstream receipt schema/validator, reproducible receipt |
| G1 | LLP 0058.000.000 protocol | Deny-all kernel tables, policy/revocation, FIFO, coordinator, hooks, acknowledgements, teardown; mock-adapter evidence |
| G2 | G0 engine/receipt plus G1 kernel | One authenticated `FactoryV1` module and the complete vertical slice through the real adapter and services |
| G3 | G2 plus authenticated graph/import machinery | Multi-module namespaces, delegation/attenuation, poisoning and unsupported-shape refusals |
| G4 | G3 plus LLP 0059.000 and reuse audits | Per-operation classification and completed, non-hybrid API families |
| G5 | G4 plus platform builds, posture machinery, and accepted governing amendments | Eligible posture/target/feature combinations with target-specific evidence |
| G6 | G0–G5, the graduation manifest, closed-world inventory, and every LLP 0058.000 §8 gate | Production eligibility for the whole artifact; default selection remains a later decision |

Partial claims are intended to be prevented by separate artifacts, final-link inventory, absence rather than fallback, whole-artifact selection, exact target evidence, and mandatory LLP 0021/0039 gates.

## Findings

### GF-ART-001 — P0 — MATERIAL: the receipt chain and final-link authority are not defined

Target citations: §5 “Artifact boundary” and “Receipt”; §6 step 1; §10.

Section 5 defines only `ibex/hermes-upstream-pinned-receipt/1`. Section 6 nevertheless requires both that engine receipt and a “greenfield build receipt (§5),” while §10 says the graduation-manifest digest is bound by “the §5 receipt.” G0 must produce a reproducible upstream receipt before G1–G5 and the graduation manifest exist.

Material decision points left open:

- Whether the greenfield build receipt is the upstream-Hermes receipt, an unnamed second receipt, or merely the graduation manifest.
- Whether “final artifact” means the Hermes library, runtime library, product executable, dynamically loaded library, or packaged executable set.
- Whether a product shipping both families uses separate executables or a common dynamic loader. The latter is reachability-equivalent to the dual-family boundary §5 says requires an amendment.
- Who performs the post-link scan and how its result is bound to the exact bytes later loaded.
- How the manifest, receipt, closure manifests, and final-artifact digest avoid a self-hash cycle.

Failure mode: an intermediate engine/runtime library can validate while a later product link introduces legacy objects or an alternate loader path. Alternatively, an implementation can invent incompatible receipt layering or create a circular, non-reproducible receipt.

Smallest sound disposition: define a non-circular receipt chain with distinct schemas and producers:

1. G0 `HermesInputReceipt`;
2. per-graph `ModuleReceipt`;
3. `GraduationManifest`;
4. a post-link `GreenfieldFinalArtifactReceipt` binding the exact final loaded artifact, linker/object/entry-root closure, prior receipt digests, and graduation-manifest digest.

Specify whether the final receipt is an external sidecar or uses an explicitly excluded embedded digest projection. Require the composition root to compare it with a product-pinned expected identity before allocation. Treat any common loader capable of reaching both families as the dual-family boundary requiring the stated amendment.

### GF-PROV-002 — P0 — MATERIAL: security-relevant compiler inputs lack exact digest domains

Target citations: §3.3; §5 “Receipt”; G0; G2; §10.

Terms including “code digest,” “graph identity,” “build/lowering authorities,” and “final source/link manifests” do not define their payloads or closed inventories. G0 is asked to design only the upstream receipt schema; no gate owns the exact module-receipt or greenfield-build-receipt schema.

Material decision points left open:

- Whether `code digest` covers source bytes, lowered factory bytes, HBC output, or only one of them.
- Whether graph identity binds every module body and edge or only package/module names.
- Whether lowering identity covers just a checked-in script or its executable, dependencies, configuration, plugins, and flags.
- Whether runtime validation cross-checks the module receipt against the loaded runtime/HBC ABI before contexts are minted.
- Whether final-link evidence binds actual input objects and flags or only a declared source list.

Failure mode: stale or substituted lowering/compiler inputs can produce authority-global lookup code or an incompatible factory while the receipt remains internally valid. An independently validating runtime cannot reconstruct the provenance claim.

Smallest sound disposition: assign exact, closed-field schemas and canonical digest projections to G0/G2. At minimum bind authenticated source bodies and graph edges, factory wrapper/version, builder and lowering closure/configuration, exact `hermesc` bytes and invocation, HBC output and ABI, runtime/link inputs, final output bytes, and every negative-scan input. Extra or missing fields must fail. Verify the graph/module receipt before §6 step 6 mints contexts or bindings.

### GF-OWN-003 — P0 — MATERIAL: runtime ownership and task acknowledgements do not yet guarantee an acyclic, reentrant construction

Target citations: §3.1–§3.4; §6 step 2; §7 item 5.

The target specifies semantic owners but not the strong-reference direction or the logical acknowledgment interface. The call graph is intentionally bidirectional: adapter/services call kernel operations, while the coordinator calls adapter/service hooks. During `invoke`, JavaScript can immediately reenter the kernel through a bound host callable.

Material decision points left open:

- Whether the composition root, kernel, or adapter strongly owns the other components. Mutual `Arc`/shared ownership satisfies the arrows but creates a teardown cycle.
- Whether the coordinator holds a kernel borrow or lock while calling `checkpoint`, `invoke`, or a service. Holding it makes ordinary host-call reentrancy deadlock or impossible.
- What exact result distinguishes “not started; rollback is safe” from “invocation began; the task is committed” and from terminal disposal.
- Whether adapter/service slot state may change independently or only through the phrase “reserve → commit/rollback acknowledgement.”
- What a teardown acknowledgment proves when a worker still holds a `CompletionLease`, and whether records may be recycled after such an acknowledgment.
- Whether the adapter’s required keyed engine-root slot table is permitted by §3.2’s literal prohibition on owning a “callback map.”

Failure mode: reference cycles prevent the fixed teardown; an exclusive kernel guard deadlocks on a normal callback; an ambiguous hook failure loses or duplicates a task; or a late completion reaches recycled storage after an over-early acknowledgment.

Smallest sound disposition: add a construction-specific logical protocol, without duplicating LLP 0058.000.000:

- Name the sole strong owner and require all reverse links to be weak, borrowed, or generation-keyed.
- Define one-time hook registration and sealing, including partial-bootstrap rollback.
- Prohibit holding kernel table/queue/teardown guards across adapter or service calls that can reenter.
- Give every reservation an opaque, exactly-once acknowledgment with `RollbackUnstarted`, `CommittedStarted`, and `TerminalDisposed` outcomes.
- Define release acknowledgment as proving that all engine/transport references are severed; late producers may retain only a safe generation tombstone.
- Clarify that an adapter root-slot table is allowed but owns no scheduling, policy, or callback lifecycle semantics.

### GF-TIER-004 — P1 — MATERIAL: non-shrinkability and product selection have no external authority

Target citations: §3.5; G4–G6; §10.

The manifest self-declares its application/API tier, permitted `Absent` entries, formats, corpus, targets, and postures. Saying that a reduction is “a different tier that re-enters G6” does not let a clean validator distinguish a legitimate new tier from a silently narrowed old one.

Material decision points left open:

- Whether the required tier floor comes from LLP 0059.000, a prior release manifest, a checked-in tier catalog, or the current manifest itself.
- Who decides which `Absent` classifications are permitted.
- Whether G5 covers all five named platforms or only targets the manifest elects to declare.
- Whether the composition root accepts any internally valid manifest or requires a product-configured tier/digest.
- Whether renaming a reduced tier is sufficient for the product to select it automatically.

Failure mode: a release can drop an API, module format, corpus member, target, or secure posture, generate a fresh internally valid manifest, rerun only the reduced G6 set, and still be selected as generic “greenfield.” This is a silent partial claim.

Smallest sound disposition: introduce an independently versioned, receipt-bound tier definition containing the immutable minimum API/format/corpus/target/posture sets and permitted absences. Same-tier successors must prove a superset relationship to a bound predecessor. Any reduction requires a new tier identity, explicit product selection and disclosure, and full G6. The product must pin the expected tier or manifest digest rather than trusting artifact self-description.

## Coherence and feasibility

The governing-contract posture is otherwise coherent:

- LLP 0021 remains fail closed: §1 explicitly blocks G6 until an accepted amendment generates greenfield coverage and advertisement cells, and §10 requires its exact conformance reports.
- LLP 0039’s secure, ambient, and insecure postures remain distinct; failure during secure bootstrap cannot select another posture.
- LLP 0013 deputy/audit and LLP 0040 extension behavior remain on the patched tier until accepted amendments exist.
- LLP 0058.000.000 retains ownership of the detailed security protocol; this target appropriately owns construction and final-artifact exclusion.

The implementation excerpts support the proposed reuse boundary:

- The pure Rust algorithms are plausible `AdoptLeaf` candidates.
- Current `HostHandle` values lack a runtime key; `call_context` accepts scalar principals; `ibex2_stdlib_runtime_new` consults the process-global Host/VFS registry.
- Current timers use `currentPrincipalId`, principal-stack collection, scalar callback handles, and a C++ timer map.
- Current filesystem installation publishes `__ibex2ReadFile` and retains direct callback/runtime pointers.
- Current Hermes provenance accepts patched or Android Maven identities and validates selected engine/link artifacts, not the final greenfield product boundary.

The target correctly classifies those paths as reference-only. The rewrite is substantial but feasible once the ownership and receipt contracts above are fixed.

## Strengths

- The artifact-level no-hybrid rule is explicit and substantially stronger than feature-disabled legacy composition.
- G0 is a genuine public-interface kill gate; private headers and local patches are not escape routes.
- The first production fixture must use the final bootstrap and coordinator, preventing a diagnostic bootstrap from becoming accidental architecture.
- The vertical slice covers reentrancy, task ordering, staged authorization, opaque resources, and teardown before broad API work.
- “Every listed entry,” exact-target reports, and the explicit LLP 0021 amendment preserve the intended fail-closed evidence posture.
