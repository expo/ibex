# Review of LLP 0071 r2 (6396901) — Grok 4.7, xhigh

Run: `grok --reasoning-effort xhigh --permission-mode plan --no-subagents --output-format streaming-json`, 2026-10-07, blind to the other review. Final message verbatim.

I'll audit revision r2 of the timers RFC against the two r1 reviews and the code it cites, staying inside this checkout.The two r1 reviews and r2 are in hand. Next I’ll check each material claim against the timer, delivery, and spec text they cite.Revision r2 is not safe to implement as written. The clock contract, the wake rule, the pre-checkpoint, and the `performance.now` amendment are real fixes. Interval reschedule is attached to the wrong take, and the drive flag still does not cover the cycle the owning pump actually runs.

## 1. Prior MATERIAL findings

**Astra 1 — clock callback under the timer lock, with no thread or panic contract.** RESOLVED. D3 says the callback runs with no Ibex lock held, names the owner thread for JavaScript, scheduling, and admission, and any thread for `is_idle` and `millis_until_next_timer`, forbids re-entry into this runtime, and catches a panic as an invalid sample (`llp/0071-timers-and-a-clock-for-a-borrowed-runtime.rfc.md:94-100`). That forces `set_timer` to sample before `timers.lock()`; today the sample is an argument evaluated while the guard is alive (`crates/ibex2/src/task.rs:1006-1011`).

**Astra 2 — non-finite clocks and `setInterval(f, 0)` do not terminate.** RESOLVED. Non-finite, negative, and above-2^53 ms samples are discarded (`rfc.md:93-95`). Repeats are at least 4 ms and are not re-queued inside one admission (`rfc.md:157-159`), so `admit_due_timers`'s loop (`task.rs:1027-1035`) cannot spin on a zero delay. The 2^53 bound is on milliseconds, not microseconds; see the new minor below.

**Astra 3 — no pre-checkpoint, no nested-delivery guard.** PARTLY. D5 puts the pre-checkpoint first (`rfc.md:125-126`). The guard is only inside `deliver_one` (`rfc.md:131-134`). LLP 0058.000.000 §8's non-idle states include both checkpoints (`llp/0058.000.000-rust-capability-context-and-engine-adapter.spec.md:449-467`). The owning pump holds the flag across them (`crates/ibex2-runtime/src/engine/hermes_shim.cc:535-545`). A borrowed caller drains microtasks in `storage_consumer_step` when `deliver` is false (`crates/ibex2-runtime/tests/embedding.cc:287-292`), outside the flag, so a host call from that drain can enter `deliver_one` and run a second task.

**Astra 4 — "admission does not wake" is false.** RESOLVED. D2 matches `CompletionQueue::admit` (`rfc.md:64-67`, `task.rs:203-208`, `230-248`).

**Astra 5 — intervals reschedule on admission, not on delivery commit.** PARTLY. D6 stops the double-queue and states one queued occurrence (`rfc.md:142-156`). The reschedule is specified at take, not at the commit LLP 0058 describes ("reservation is not an irreversible timer transition", then commit, and only then reschedule; `0058.000.000:453-462`, `551-556`). The take it names is the wrong function. Detail under finding A.

**Astra 6 — `performance.now` may not be Ibex's, and LLP 0059.000 §2 is not amended.** RESOLVED for this review. D4 replaces any earlier `performance.now` under `TIMERS` and rewrites §2 as a host read of the runtime clock (`rfc.md:112-120`). `scripts()` evaluates `timers` before `events` (`crates/ibex2/src/bindings.rs:347-358`), so `events.js:30-33` captures the replacement. The parent surface spec is Grok's item, not this one.

**Grok 1 — construction-time timer runs before the microtasks that construction queued.** RESOLVED. Same text as Astra 3's first half (`rfc.md:125-126`). `setTimeout(t, 0)` is due immediately (`crates/ibex2/src/stdlib/timers.rs:65-68`, `227-232`); the pre-checkpoint runs the reaction that clears it before admission.

**Grok 2 — admission of the forwarded method does wake.** RESOLVED. Same as Astra 4.

**Grok 3 — no lock, thread, or re-entry contract, and the monotonic floor keeps an `Instant` sample from before `set_clock`.** RESOLVED as written. The contract is D3 (`rfc.md:96-100`). Sealing at the first read, including `is_idle`, refuses `set_clock` after that sample (`rfc.md:80-83`), so a caller clock at 0 is not floored by an earlier `Instant` read if nothing has read yet. `RuntimeState::new` stores `started` and does not call `now()` (`task.rs:542`). A concurrent `is_idle` during `set_clock` is a separate hole (finding E).

**Grok 4 — `deliver_one` does not take the drive flag.** PARTLY. D5 takes it (`rfc.md:131-134`). It does not span the checkpoints, and it turns the owning pump's quiet nested return into a logic error. Detail under finding B.

**Grok 5 — due-ness and the sleep value disagree, so a held clock busy-spins.** RESOLVED, provided the sleep is computed from the same integer microseconds as the due check (`rfc.md:84-88`). `run_to_quiescence` treats 0 as "pump again" (`crates/ibex2-runtime/src/engine/hermes.rs:960-969`). Integer comparison makes those the same predicate. Converting both sides back through `f64` before subtracting does not; the RFC's 2^53 cap does not prevent that (finding E).

**Grok 6 — LLP 0059.000 §2 and LLP 0059 §3.** PARTLY. `0059.000` §2 is amended (`rfc.md:117-120`). `llp/0059-standard-library-surface-v1.spec.md:66` ("must share the frame clock's time base") and `:83-85` (`requestAnimationFrame` is the host's frame clock) are not named. D4's sentence that a frame clock takes its base from this clock is the reconciliation, but it is not an amendment of that spec.

**Grok 7 — LLP 0058 §8's cycle, which the RFC said did not change, and LLP 0068's wake paragraph.** PARTLY. The wake half is resolved: D2 agrees with `llp/0068-the-standard-library-for-a-rust-consumer.spec.md:339-347` instead of contradicting it. The cycle half is the same gap as Astra 3 and Grok 4. §8 says a nested drive records a wakeup and returns (`0058.000.000:466-467`); the pump does that by returning `OK` when `begin_drive` fails (`hermes_shim.cc:537-538`). D5 makes the same flag a logic error on `deliver_one`.

## 2. New findings

**A. MATERIAL — reschedule-on-take is attached to "the adapter", and that is not where the owning runtime takes the task.** Both drivers call `ibex2_take_task` (`crates/ibex2/src/boundary_abi.rs:1216`). The owning pump calls it at `hermes_shim.cc:555` and then `fire_timer` at `:562`. It never calls `Adapter::deliver_one` (`crates/ibex2/src/bindings/install.cc:2308`). `RuntimeState::take_task` (`task.rs:1155-1166`) pops under the subscription lock and drops that lock when it returns, so the clock read can sit in `ibex2_take_task` after `take_task()` and still satisfy D3. Putting it only in `deliver_one`, which is what "when the adapter takes a timer task" says (`rfc.md:152-153`), drops the interval out of the wheel inside `admit_due_timers` (`task.rs:1024-1036`, where `take_due` currently reschedules at `timers.rs:120-124`) and never puts it back on the pump path. `an_interval_repeats_until_cleared` then stops at one tick (`crates/ibex2-runtime/src/engine/hermes_tests.rs:2555-2566`). D6's "the owning runtime gets the same behavior; nothing it relies on changes" (`rfc.md:161`) does not name this call. The wheel tests `an_interval_reschedules_itself` and `a_late_interval_does_not_fire_a_backlog` (`timers.rs:203-224`) assert that `take_due` itself reschedules; D6 makes those fail, and section 4 does not mention them.

**B. MATERIAL — `deliver_one` taking the drive flag does not implement the cycle, and it changes the storage entry point the RFC still points at that flag.** `begin_drive` / `end_drive` are one boolean (`task.rs:985-991`). The pump sets it for the whole cycle and clears it with `DriveGuard` on every exit, including a deadline abort (`hermes_shim.cc:238-244`, `537-540`). `end_drive` stores `false` with no owner check. `deliver_one` is what a storage caller uses (`embedding.cc:290`); `caller_owns_checkpoints_and_storage_is_typed_and_granted` depends on that call not draining microtasks (`embedding.rs:1986-1992`). D5 holds the flag only for `deliver_one`. That refuses a timer callback which calls `deliver_one` again, which is the case test 3 states. It does not refuse a microtask in the caller's checkpoint, which is the case §8 forbids. It also refuses a settlement callback that calls `deliver_one` again. No current storage test does that, so the suite does not go red, and the RFC never says the storage path changes. The owning pump stays correct only because it does not call `deliver_one`. Sharing the flag is an interlock, not a second acquisition, as long as `deliver_one` does not call `end_drive` when `begin_drive` returned false. D5 does not say that.

**C. The 4 ms floor is implementable and does not by itself break a named test.** The normative rule is `max(delay, 4 ms)` on repeats, first occurrence at the given delay (`rfc.md:157-159`). `an_interval_repeats_until_cleared` uses 5 ms, so it still fires three times inside 120 ms if finding A is implemented on the pump path. `MIN_DELAY` stays 0 for `setTimeout` (`timers.rs:65-68`), which is what D5's "a `setTimeout(f, 0)` chain is due forever" needs (`rfc.md:139-140`). The parenthetical "HTML's clamp, which every interval reaches after its fifth run" is the nesting rule HTML applies after five deep timers, not a 4 ms floor on every repeat. Test 4 follows the floor, not the parenthetical. See finding F.

**D. Replacing `performance.now` under `TIMERS` does not break the owning runtime's clock tests.** Without `set_clock`, the source remains `Instant` (`rfc.md:77-78`). `performance_now_advances_and_shares_the_runtime_origin` (`hermes_tests.rs:2642-2658`) and the deadline spin (`crates/ibex2-runtime/src/engine/hermes_deadline_tests.rs:25-31`) already observe a runtime-relative clock that moves with wall time; Ibex's host op is already that clock when `performance.now` is absent (`timers.js:96-101`, `boundary_abi.rs:320`). Unconditional replacement is a no-op on that realm. `events.js` runs after `timers.js`, so `timeStamp` follows the new function. Integer microseconds quantize the value; a 20 ms sleep still satisfies `second - first >= 15`. No existing test installs a `performance.now` and expects it to survive `TIMERS`.

**E. MINOR — the atomic maximum and the seal are implementable, and two edges are open.** A mutex that is dropped before the callback, plus `AtomicU64::fetch_max` for the returned sample, meets "no Ibex lock" and "atomic maximum; no mutex" (`rfc.md:89-90`). `catch_unwind` has to live inside `now()`: `ibex2_host_call` is `extern "C"` and does not catch (`boundary_abi.rs:514-520`). That is doable, and it keeps the panic off the C++ boundary. Not specified: the value of the first sample when the clock is invalid or panics and the atomic is still 0. Also, `is_idle` may run on another thread while `set_clock` runs (`rfc.md:98-99`). A read that has loaded the `Instant` source but not yet sealed can `fetch_max` that sample after `set_clock` has published the caller clock, which is the r1 poisoning bug as a race. The 2^53 cap is on milliseconds (`rfc.md:93-94`). Microseconds stop being exact `f64` integers at 2^53 microseconds, about 104 days. Due and sleep agree only if both stay in integer arithmetic. `(deadline_us as f64 / 1000.0) - (now_us as f64 / 1000.0)` is 0 for distinct integers in that range, and `hermes.rs:967` then busy-spins.

**F. MINOR — the HTML citation will be implemented as the wrong clamp if someone follows it instead of test 4.** "After its fifth run" is not "every repeat is at least 4 ms". The bullet and test 4 agree with each other. The parenthetical does not.

## 3. The eleven tests

1. **Correct.** At 0, deadlines are 10, 20, and 50 ms, so admission is empty, including after real time passes on a held clock. At 25 the due order is `g` then `h`. `h` is taken at 25 and rescheduled by 20 ms to 45 (`timers.rs:120-124` moved to take time). At 60 the due order is `h` (45) then `f` (50). `clearInterval` in that first `h` runs after the reschedule, so the 45 ms occurrence is removed and nothing is admitted later. This repairs the r1 expectation.

2. **Correct.** The pre-checkpoint runs the reaction that clears `t` before admission, so `t` never runs. `deliver_one` runs one callback and does not drain (`install.cc:2308-2349`, `embedding.rs:1986-1992`).

3. **Correct for the nested call, incomplete for the queue.** The refusal has to happen before `ibex2_take_task`, or the next task is already gone. "The queue is unchanged" means the nested call does not pop. The outer call has already popped the running timer.

4. **Not correct as stated.** "Admitting twice before delivering queues one occurrence" is right: the second admit does not see the interval, because it is off the wheel until take. "`setInterval(f, 0)` admits once per cycle and then not again until the clock moves 4 ms" contradicts itself. The first cycle admits the delay-0 occurrence and take schedules the next at +4 ms. Later cycles at a held clock admit nothing. It does not admit once per cycle.

5. **Correct as a property.** A backward step does not move deadlines or `performance.now`. NaN, infinity, a negative value, and a panic keep the previous accepted sample. A non-integer sample makes `millis_until_next_timer() == 0` line up with admission only when both use that integer. The test does not give the sample or the deadline, so it does not catch finding E's `f64` subtraction.

6. **Correct for the three calls it names.** `is_idle` reads time (`task.rs:995-998`). A second `set_clock` is refused. Deadlines already stored stay put because `set_clock` cannot switch domains. It does not cover `millis_until_next_timer` alone, which is also a read (`task.rs:1041-1047`).

7. **Right property, no expected result.** "Not a deadlock" matches D3 only if the clock runs after every Ibex lock is dropped, including the subscription lock in `take_task`. The contract also says the clock must not call into this runtime (`rfc.md:99-100`). The test requires that forbidden call and does not say whether the `setTimeout` is queued, rejected, or recurses until the stack overflows.

8. **Correct only with `EVENTS` installed, which the test does not say.** `reportException` dispatches a cancelable `error` and calls the host reporter only when dispatch returns true (`events.js:445-458`, `417`). `preventDefault` makes dispatch return `!canceled`. The next `deliver_one` still runs because the catch in `deliver_one` does not drop the queue. Without `EVENTS`, `report_error` calls `ibex2_report_uncaught` directly (`install.cc:2295-2305`) and `preventDefault` cannot suppress it. The test also forces `end_drive` on the throw path; D5 never says the flag is cleared when `fire_timer` throws. A leaked bit refuses every later cycle (`hermes_deadline_tests.rs:50-52`).

9. **Correct**, given `timers` before `events` and `set_clock` before the first read. The captured `performance.now` calls the host op, so `timeStamp` (`events.js:97-99`) tracks the caller clock, including when a previous `performance.now` was replaced.

10. **Not correct as stated.** "Today's behavior on `Instant`" is false. D6's take-time reschedule and the 4 ms floor apply with no `set_clock`. `setInterval(fn, 0)` currently hangs inside `admit_due_timers`; afterward it repeats every 4 ms of `Instant`. The two `timers.rs` interval tests change with it.

11. **Right scenario, not a complete statement.** `CONSOLE | PURE | EVENTS | TIMERS | WEBSOCKET` passes `Groups::validate` (`bindings.rs:157-163`). Denial as `error` then `close(1006)` is the existing borrowed path (`embedding.rs:584-635`) and still works if no timer is queued ahead of those events. The echo, the timer reconnect, and "driven only by the cycle" are new requirements, not wrong ones. The test does not say `#[cfg(feature = "websocket")]`, and it does not call `prepare_garbage_collection` even though D7 requires that before an explicit collection (`rfc.md:168-170`, `hermes_shim.cc:491-496`, `embedding.cc:295-298` still collects without it).

**Sufficient?** No. Missing, and able to hide the bugs above:

- The owning pump's take reschedules an interval, and `timers.rs`'s `take_due` tests are updated or replaced. Section 4 only adds borrowed-fixture tests.
- `clearInterval` after admission and before take delivers the queued occurrence as the `timers.js` no-op (`timers.js:58-63`, `69-71`) and does not reschedule. Test 1 clears during the callback, after take. Test 2 clears before admission.
- A storage settlement and a timer in one FIFO, through the same `deliver_one` the storage loop uses (`embedding.cc:290`), including a settlement callback that calls `deliver_one` again.
- The drive flag released when the callback throws, and a microtask in the caller's checkpoint unable to start a second host task.
- `prepare_garbage_collection` before the borrowed collector.

The integer clock, the seal, the panic catch, the 4 ms repeat floor, and unconditional `performance.now` replacement are implementable. The reschedule site and the drive-flag span are not, as written, against this tree.
